/who-i-am

I do not fit neatly into one box and stopped trying to a long time ago. My background is mechanical engineering, but I think like a product person and operate like a builder. I graduated summa cum laude from Embry-Riddle while playing collegiate baseball, running side businesses, and carrying a full course load. Baseball gave me a performance mindset. Train, measure, adjust, execute. Engineering gave me a systems mindset. Understand the mechanism, find the constraints, improve the design. AI gave me a leverage mindset. One sharp person with the right tools can do the work of many. All three are running in parallel. I learn by touching the tool, breaking the workflow, and seeing what happens. Not by reading theory and waiting until I feel ready.

My brain works pattern-first. I am constantly reducing chaos into signal, and that instinct does not care what domain it is in. When I look at a dataset, I want to know what is actually happening underneath the numbers. When I look at a career decision, a market, or a piece of theology, I am always asking the same questions. What is the real mechanism here, what actually matters, and what can be built or improved from that understanding. I am especially drawn to places where different worlds collide, AI and sports science, finance and automation, design and technical storytelling, because that intersection is usually where the hidden structure lives. The interesting problems are almost always at the edges.

I like the early stage of things. The part where something is underdeveloped, high-potential, and ambiguous, and you get to look at it and say there is something here. Then the part where you start turning that vague sense into something visible and real. A workflow, a product, a demo, a system, a clothing drop, a page. Creation is part of how I think. Moving fast helps me think faster. I am not someone who waits until conditions are perfect. I experiment, generate output, see what lands, and adjust. That loop, fast and iterative and hands-on, is where I do my best work.

My faith shapes how I carry all of it. Not in a way that constrains the ambition, but in a way that orients it. I want to build things that matter, not just things that look good on a metrics dashboard. Ambitious, but not for status alone. Disciplined, not just for output. I want to become the kind of person who can be trusted with whatever is in front of him, and use what I am genuinely good at in a way that actually means something.
